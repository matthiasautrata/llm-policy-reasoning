# Style sheet

Patterns that mark text as model-written. Remove them; keep the content.

- Bold lead-in labels opening paragraphs ("What we tested.", "Bottom line.").
- Parallel triads of headers or bullets ("What you get / What it takes / What it avoids").
- A short verdict sentence closing a paragraph ("That is the business case.").
- "Not X, but Y" and "X is not enough" constructions, more than once per document.
- Every paragraph the same length; every section opening with a one-sentence thesis.
- Lists where the content is an argument; argument belongs in sentences.
- Signposting ("In this section", "Let us", "It is worth noting", "Importantly").
- Hedges with no information ("We believe", "arguably", "in many cases").
- Tidy symmetry: every concept with exactly three properties, every comparison with exactly two sides.

What to aim for instead: varied sentence length; one concrete case before the general claim; first person
plural with a stated position; terms defined once, where first used; numbers with their denominators.

## Models of voice

- Leslie Lamport: plain words, short sentences, every claim stated precisely enough to be checked; say what is
  not claimed.
- Bertrand Meyer: crisp definitions, a stated position, no hedging where the evidence is clear.
- Alexis King: the piece is built around one mental shift, argued through a worked example, not a feature tour.
